Healthcare AI operates in a high-stakes regulatory environment where errors affect financial solvency, institutional accreditation, and patient privacy. Grounded in Item 13 of our approved Topic Form, we evaluate four primary risk vectors:

**•	Risk 1 — Clinical Miscoding & Upcoding/Undercoding Risk:** If the LLM hallucinates a code or misinterprets a negated/historical condition (e.g., coding 'family history of ischemic heart disease' or 'ruled out bowel perforation' as an active complication) and a hurried coder accepts it, the hospital faces either **TPA claim rejection, fraud/upcoding audits,** or corrupted clinical epidemiology records (CMS ICD-10 Guidelines).

**•	Risk 2 — Patient Data Privacy & Regulatory Breach under India's DPDP Act 2023:** Discharge summaries contain highly sensitive Protected Health Information (PHI) and Digital Personal Data—including HIV/STD status, psychiatric history, reproductive procedures, and identity numbers. Under the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023 and the notified MeitY DPDP Rules 2025, hospitals act as **Data Fiduciaries** subject to penalties up to ₹250 Crore for unauthorized data leakage or processing personal data outside approved boundaries without technical safeguards.

**•	Risk 3 — Automation Bias & Human-in-the-Loop Erosion Over Time:** As Chart Ready AI demonstrates high accuracy on routine cases, coders and deficiency officers may begin accepting AI outputs reflexively without reading the clinical text—turning a nominal HITL safeguard into a rubber stamp.

**•	Risk 4 — Algorithmic Triage Bias:** If the triage algorithm prioritizes solely by commercial TPA claim value, cash-paying or government welfare scheme (ABDM / PM-JAY) patients could experience systematic delays in discharge record completion, delaying their outpatient follow-up summaries or medico-legal certificates.
