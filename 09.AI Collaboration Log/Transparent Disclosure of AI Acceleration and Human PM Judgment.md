|2. Where AI Helped in the PM Process|• **Discovery & Taxonomy Mapping:** Rapidly cross-referencing WHO ICD-10-CM 2025/2026 surgical code families (K35–K80, E11, L97) and summarizing NABH 6th Edition ACC-12/13 discharge documentation standards.<br>• **Artifact Drafting & Structuring:** Generating initial RICE scoring tables and formatting multi-column PRD failure-mode matrices.<br>• **Prototype Scaffolding:** Writing boilerplate Tailwind CSS and JavaScript event listeners for the 3-pane interactive demonstrator.|
|3. Three Representative Prompts / Workflows Used|• **Prompt 1 (Workflow & Failure-Mode Discovery):** *'Act as a Health Information Management auditor in an NABH-accredited Indian hospital. Compare deterministic regex/EHR checkbox validation against LLM semantic checking on General Surgery discharge summaries. Identify 5 concrete clinical phrasing examples where keyword rules fail but human-in-the-loop NLP succeeds.'*<br>• **Prompt 2 (PRD Guardrail & Edge-Case Stress Test):** *'Review our draft PRD for Chart Ready AI (scoped to top 50 General Surgery ICD-10 codes). Identify failure modes related to intra-operative ruled-out diagnoses, Excludes1 coding conflicts, poor OCR scans, and coder automation bias, and propose measurable go/no-go thresholds.'*<br>• **Prompt 3 (Interactive HITL Prototype Generation):** *'Build a single-file browser prototype (HTML/Tailwind/JS) for Chart Ready AI featuring a 3-pane layout: (1) Risk-prioritized MRD queue, (2) Discharge summary viewer with 5-field NABH completeness check and highlighted text spans, and (3) ICD-10 suggestion cards with Accept/Edit/Reject buttons, an override reason modal, and a locked billing export gate.'*|
|4. Where AI Was Weak, Generic, or Wrong|• **Flaw 1 — Unrealistic '95% Zero-Touch Auto-Coding' Claims:** Initial raw LLM drafts suggested auto-submitting high-confidence (>90%) charts directly to TPA billing without human coder review to maximize ROI.<br>• **Flaw 2 — US-Centric Regulatory & Billing Hallucinations:** Generic AI outputs repeatedly cited US HIPAA, Medicare MS-DRGs, and CPT codes rather than India's **DPDP Act 2023**, **NABH 6th Edition**, and Indian **TPA cashless claim workflows**.<br>• **Flaw 3 — Over-Broad MVP Scope:** Early AI roadmap suggestions proposed launching across all hospital departments (70,000+ ICD-10 codes) simultaneously with live bi-directional EMR integration in Month 1.<br>• **Flaw 4 — Ignoring Physician Sign-Off Authority:** An early AI workflow draft suggested having the LLM auto-generate missing discharge follow-up instructions for patients.|
|5. Human Corrections & PM Judgment Applied|• **Correction 1 — Enforced 100% HITL Gate (Zero Auto-Billing):** Overrode the AI's auto-submission suggestion; instituted our core rule that *no AI-suggested code is ever posted to a bill unless a coder accepts, edits, or rejects it first*.<br>• **Correction 2 — Scoped Narrow Wedge to General Surgery Top 30–50 Codes:** Restricted the MVP to one department and one document type to contain clinical variance and eliminate cold-start training dependencies.<br>• **Correction 3 — Hard-Blocked AI Clinical Authoring:** Mandated that deficiency flags routed to clinicians must be completed and signed solely by the attending physician.<br>• **Correction 4 — Localized Governance & Economics:** Grounded all privacy controls in India's DPDP Act 2023 (hospital VPC + local PHI masking) and modeled realistic INR unit economics (₹110 baseline vs. ₹46.20 assisted cost).|
|6. Final Reflection: Accelerator vs. PM Reasoning|AI tools reduced drafting, coding, and visual diagramming time by ~60%, allowing rapid iteration on our interactive prototype. However, **AI could not replace PM judgment** on five critical decisions: (1) narrowing the first wedge to General Surgery, (2) rejecting autonomous billing due to asymmetric clinical/financial error costs, (3) designing anti-complacency guardrails for human coders, (4) choosing an API-first VPC path over custom model training, and (5) setting a conservative 'Pilot-First' launch recommendation.|
